# Hybrid deductive-inductive codebook

This codebook combines deductive sensitizing concepts with descriptive
categories refined during coding of the time-stratified sample.

## Deductive sensitizing concepts

These concepts guide attention but are not mutually exclusive outcome classes.

| Code | Sensitizing concept | Operational prompt |
|---|---|---|
| D-PC | Finite pool of concern | Does one crisis compete with or displace attention, urgency, resources, or policy priority assigned to the other? |
| D-AG | Affect generalization / emotional coupling | Does language or interpretation developed around one crisis spill over to the other? What form does the linkage take? |
| D-TIME | Acute versus chronic threat | Does the comment compare immediacy, duration, visibility, mortality, or future severity? |
| D-SCI | Epistemic authority | Are evidence, expertise, scientific institutions, denial, misinformation, or politicization used to connect the crises? |
| D-GOV | Governance and collective capacity | Are government performance, public cooperation, mobilization, spending, regulation, or institutional learning compared? |
| D-SYS | Shared structural drivers | Are ecological degradation, political economy, inequality, production, consumption, or other common drivers invoked? |

## Descriptive categories

| Code | Category | Include when | Do not use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Crisis comparison and attention competition | The comment compares severity, urgency, time horizon, salience, attention, or resources; one crisis may serve as preview or analogy for the other. | The crises are merely listed without an interpretable comparison. |
| C2 | Causal links and shared drivers | The comment claims a material link between the crises or identifies common ecological, economic, production, or consumption drivers. | Similar political reactions are the main connection; consider C3 or C4. |
| C3 | Science, misinformation, and politicization | Expertise, evidence, denial, conspiracy, media framing, partisan identity, or politicization is the organizing link. | The central concern is institutional action rather than knowledge claims; consider C4. |
| C4 | Governance, collective action, and policy capacity | Government performance, cooperation, mobilization, regulation, spending, or policy lessons connect the crises. | Broad socioeconomic consequences dominate; consider C5. |
| C5 | Social consequences, inequality, and future transition | Livelihoods, unequal vulnerability, social stress, green recovery, generational burden, collapse, or systemic transition form the connection. | The comment identifies an upstream common driver rather than downstream consequences; consider C2. |
| OTHER_NEW | Inductively generated category | A coherent crisis connection cannot be represented without distorting C1-C5. Record a proposed label, definition, and at least two supporting instances before elevating it to a category. | The problem is only uncertainty between existing categories. |
| O0 | Enumerative or rhetorical co-mention | Both crises appear in a list, joke, insult, aside, or rhetorical scenery without a sufficiently interpretable connection. | A weak but interpretable connection is present. |
| UNCERTAIN | Unresolved boundary | Available text suggests a connection but the primary interpretation cannot yet be assigned consistently. | The text is unusable; use EX. |
| EX | Exclude | The retained comment is malformed, empty, quotation-only without interpretable context, or otherwise unusable. Record the reason. | A disagreeable, sarcastic, factually incorrect, or uncommon opinion is still interpretable. |

## Multiple coding

One primary category captures the organizing connection. A secondary category
is used only when removing it would materially misrepresent the comment. Open
codes and deductive sensitizing concepts may be multiple. Sarcasm and factual
accuracy are recorded in the memo; they do not by themselves determine a code.

## Framework-integration values

Use one of the following in `relation_to_quantitative_results`:

- `SUPPORTS`: gives a plausible interpretive expression consistent with a
  quantitative pattern;
- `EXTENDS`: adds a dimension not captured by the quantitative measures;
- `QUALIFIES`: narrows, conditions, or complicates the quantitative reading;
- `CONTRADICTS`: provides a meaningful negative/deviant interpretation;
- `NOT_DIRECTLY_RELATED`: does not illuminate a reported quantitative result.

These labels record how each coded comment relates to the quantitative findings.
